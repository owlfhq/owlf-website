# owlf Website Copy Guidelines

Use this standard for every customer-facing copy review in this repository. Apply it to existing text before writing new text.

## Decision system: KEEP, REWRITE, REMOVE

For each text element, assign one decision:

- **KEEP** if it is clear, human, accurate, useful in its current location, and not repeated nearby.
- **REWRITE** if the information matters but the phrasing is awkward, generic, overly technical, repetitive, hesitant, or not in the owlf brand voice.
- **REMOVE** if it describes internal implementation, repeats another section, labels a mock-up/test as if the customer needs that explanation, fills space without helping a customer, or makes an unsupported promise.
- If a detail is important but belongs elsewhere, **MOVE** it to the FAQ or to the relevant in-product instruction instead of deleting the fact.

## Voice

- Brand name is **owlf** in customer-facing copy (lowercase).
- Sound like a practical, friendly person helping with a home job in Canada.
- Prefer concrete everyday language: dripping tap, blocked drain, move-out clean, share your area.
- Use Canadian spelling where applicable (e.g. neighbourhood, shovelling).
- Keep headings short and specific. Do not force every heading to be a slogan.
- The chosen brand line is **“A home job? Start with ‘Hi.’”**
- Do not use generic startup/AI phrases such as “made easier,” “check what’s possible,” “the next step for your area,” or abstract claims about “a simple first step.”
- Avoid filler, rhetorical padding, exclamation marks, and repeated explanations.

## Accuracy and product limits

- Do not promise provider availability, a successful match, response time, service coverage, fixed pricing, reviews, or guaranteed completion.
- Avoid placing uncertainty in headlines and step titles. Put necessary limitations in a concise FAQ answer.
- The current website links create a WhatsApp draft without a recipient number. The visitor chooses a recipient before sending. Never claim the link opens a direct conversation with owlf until a destination number is configured.
- Do not present demo content or simulated conversations as live activity. Prefer removing a fake chat rather than adding long disclaimers around it.
- Do not expose internal terms such as “current WhatsApp flow,” “customer browse menu,” “category acknowledgement,” or implementation notes in public copy.

## Every section must have one job

- Hero: establish the brand idea and invite a first message.
- Service catalogue: help visitors find a category and understand common jobs.
- Steps: explain the customer action in order, without repeating the hero.
- General explanatory section: answer a real customer hesitation; otherwise remove it.
- FAQ: keep necessary qualifications and practical details, including WhatsApp recipient selection and variable pricing.
- CTA: state the action, not the underlying implementation.
- Remove repeated claims from multiple sections. If a sentence does not help a visitor choose, act, or understand a necessary limitation, remove it.

## Copy review checklist

1. Is the sentence written for a customer rather than a developer or product owner?
2. Would a person naturally say it aloud?
3. Does it tell the visitor something new and useful in this section?
4. Does it sound hesitant or imply OWLF cannot help before the visitor has asked?
5. Does it promise something the current product cannot guarantee?
6. Is the same fact already stated nearby?
7. Is any implementation caveat better placed in the FAQ?
8. Does the wording fit Canadian English and the lowercase owlf brand?

Do not publish until all eight questions have been considered.
